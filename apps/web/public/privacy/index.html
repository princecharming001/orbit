Orbit

Privacy policy

Effective November 12, 2026

Orbit (tryorbit.today) helps students find people worth a coffee chat, write to them, and keep track of each conversation. This page says what Orbit reads, what it keeps, who helps run it, and how to delete it. Nothing is ever sent from your accounts without your approval of the exact message.

What Orbit reads, and only when you connect it

  • Your Google account, when you sign in with Google:
    • Gmail (read): messages with the people you network with, to keep track of who you talked to, notice replies, and write follow-ups that fit the conversation.
    • Gmail (send): to send the messages you approve, from your own address.
    • Google Calendar: your events, to know when a chat is booked, prepare you for it, and put chats you schedule on your calendar.
    • Google Contacts and other contacts: names and email addresses, so people are matched correctly.
    • Your name, email address and profile picture, to create your account.
  • LinkedIn, when you connect it: your connections, the profiles and public activity of people you look at, and your LinkedIn messages with them, to find who you know and the warmest way to reach someone.
  • Instagram, only when Orbit offers it and you connect it, through Unipile, signed in as you: who you follow, who follows you, and your chats. For chats with people you are reaching out to through Orbit, the words are kept; for every other chat, only when a message was sent and which way, never the words. To find who could introduce you to someone, Orbit reads that person's public follower and following lists. Instagram does not allow automation: Orbit reads far more than it writes, stays under Unipile's limits, and never sends a message without your tap.
  • X, only when Orbit offers it and you agree on the screen before signing in, with X's own sign-in: who you follow, who follows you back, and your last 30 days of X messages. Nothing you post, and Orbit never posts. Checking someone on X costs money, so Orbit shows the price first and reads nothing until you say go.
  • Texts, only on a Mac, when Orbit offers it and you pick your Messages file: who you text and how often. The words never leave your browser. With an Orbit account, Orbit's server gets, per person and per day, how many texts went each way, and for a friend only in your texts their name and number.
  • What you give Orbit: your answers during setup, your resume if you upload one, notes you write, and meeting notes from a notetaker you connect (Fathom, Granola, Fireflies or Wispr Flow).

How Orbit uses Google user data

Orbit's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

  • Google user data is used only to provide the features you see in Orbit: tracking your conversations, drafting and sending the messages you approve, and preparing you for chats.
  • Orbit reads the tone of replies people send you (in Gmail and on LinkedIn) to estimate how close you are, and shows that only to you. It keeps a number and short labels such as "offered to help", never a copy of the words for this. You can turn it off in Settings, under Closeness.
  • Nothing derived from Gmail or Calendar is included in what a friend on Orbit sees of your network; the closeness word a friend sees is computed from LinkedIn, your own labels and your own answers only.
  • It is never sold, never used for advertising, and never used to train AI models.
  • It is shared with no one except the services below that are needed to run those features, as required by law, or to keep Orbit secure.
  • No person at Orbit reads it, unless you ask us to for support, it is needed to investigate abuse or a security problem, or the law requires it.

Who helps run Orbit

  • Supabase stores your account data and runs Orbit's server.
  • Anthropic (Claude) writes drafts, answers your questions in the chat, and summarises conversations. It receives only what a task needs and does not use it to train its models.
  • Unipile connects your LinkedIn and Instagram accounts when you choose to.
  • X provides your X follows and messages when you sign in with X.
  • Google provides Gmail, Calendar and Contacts when you sign in with Google.
  • GitHub Pages serves the website.
  • Only if you connect or turn them on: Fathom, Granola, Fireflies and Wispr Flow (your meeting notes), Cal.com (your scheduling link), and Village (warm introduction paths; it sees the names you look up).

Orbit uses no advertising or analytics trackers.

Friends on Orbit

With an account you can pool your network with a friend who also uses Orbit, once you both accept. A friend sees the name, title, company and public LinkedIn profile of the people you choose to share, your school, whether they are your alum, and how well you know them in a word. A friend never sees your emails, notes, drafts, scores, phone numbers, email addresses, anyone you marked private or hid, or anyone who asked not to be contacted; a contact's research brief is never part of it. You choose what you share (everyone, only people at some companies, or nothing yet), you can see exactly what a friend sees, and stopping takes effect at once.

Your texts and X are never part of what a friend sees. Who you follow on Instagram is used to find paths for a friend only if you turn on "Let my Orbit friends find paths through who I follow on Instagram", which is off until you do. What you follow on X is never shared, because X's rules do not allow it.

People you look up

When you ask for Deep research on someone, Orbit reads public professional sources about them (their LinkedIn profile and public web pages), and writes a short brief with every line cited. Briefs are kept for 90 days. Public search results may be reused across students for up to 7 days, and public LinkedIn profile and activity reads for up to 30 days (a profile photo for up to 400 days), so the same person is not looked up again and again. Public Instagram and X follow lists are kept as account ids only, for up to 30 days, and are dropped when an account turns private. Orbit never keeps anyone's contact details from those shared reads. Anyone can ask to be left out of research at tryorbit.today/remove-me.

What Orbit keeps, and for how long

  • Without an Orbit account, your data stays in your own browser. Clearing the browser's site data, or Settings, Delete all data, removes it.
  • With an Orbit account, your data is kept on Orbit's server while your account is open.
  • Disconnecting Google or LinkedIn in Settings stops Orbit from reading it. What was already brought in stays until you delete it.
  • Instagram, X and Texts each have one switch in Settings. Off stops reading at once and keeps what was taken, hidden and left out of everything. Remove what Orbit took deletes it, including your rows in the shared follow graph. An Instagram account left off for 21 days is signed out at Unipile. X data expires after 30 days.
  • Deleting your account (Settings, Account) revokes Orbit's Google and X access, disconnects LinkedIn and Instagram, and deletes your data from Orbit's server, your rows in the shared follow graph included.
  • You can download everything Orbit keeps about you from Settings at any time.

Security

Connections are encrypted in transit. Access tokens for your accounts are stored encrypted and are never shown in the app. Account data is separated per user on the server.

Students under 18

Orbit is for college students and recent graduates, and is not meant for anyone under 18.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, Orbit will say so in the app before the change applies.
