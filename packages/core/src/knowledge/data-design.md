# Data science, analytics and design recruiting

Sources: apps/web/src/engine/prep.ts (data and design question banks); the firms' university programs pages; the AIGA and Nielsen Norman Group on design portfolios. Checked 2026-10-31. Posting dates change every year.

Data roles. Data scientist, data analyst, machine learning engineer and applied scientist internships post on the tech calendar, August to October for the following summer at large companies, rolling at startups. [CHANGES YEARLY: search the web for a company's current internship posting before stating when it opens.] Titles mean different things at different companies: at some, "data scientist" is analytics and SQL and experimentation; at others it is modelling and production code. Research scientist and applied scientist roles usually want a master's or PhD. The process is a resume screen, an online assessment or take-home (SQL, Python, statistics, sometimes a small modelling task), one or two technical screens (probability, A/B testing, SQL on the spot, a case about a metric that moved), and a loop with a behavioural round. Companies want evidence the student can turn a messy question into an analysis that changes a decision: a project with real data and a conclusion, an internship with a number attached, a Kaggle result only as a footnote.

What to ask a data person: where their work shows up in product or business decisions; how their time splits between analysis, modelling and building pipelines; what a data intern usually owns over a summer; which tools or skills they had to pick up fastest; what they look for in students who want to do data work on their team.

Design roles. Product design and UX internships post on a similar calendar at big tech, often a little later (September to November) and with fewer seats; agencies and startups hire closer to the start date. The portfolio is the application: three or four case studies that show the problem, the process, the trade-offs and the shipped result, with the student's own role made clear, hosted on a simple site. A resume without a portfolio link is not read. The process is a portfolio review, a design exercise or take-home, a portfolio presentation to the team, a whiteboard or app critique session, and a behavioural round. Companies want evidence of judgment and of working with engineers and PMs, not visual polish alone.

What to ask a designer: how design works with product and engineering on their team; what a design intern owns and how their work is reviewed; what they wish more student portfolios showed; what they learned in the first year that school did not prepare them for; what makes a student portfolio stand out to them.

Who to talk to for both: analysts, scientists or designers one to three years in, last summer's interns, alumni who switched into the field. Managers are asked what they look for and how they scope intern projects so the work ships.

Register: tech register. "Hi" and first name, contractions, five to seven sentences, a subject that says what the email is. Proof beats adjectives: one link to a project or a portfolio. Sign with first name. One bump at five to six days, then stop. LinkedIn works well for designers, who are active there; email for analysts when the address can be found.

Preparation for data: daily SQL and probability practice for six to eight weeks, two or three projects the student can explain end to end including what went wrong, and the statistics behind A/B tests cold. For design: the portfolio first, a practised twenty minute walk-through of the strongest case study, and a critique of the target company's product ready for every conversation.
