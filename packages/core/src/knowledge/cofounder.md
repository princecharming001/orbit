# Finding a co-founder

Sources: Y Combinator's co-founder matching guide and essays on co-founder relationships; First Round on founder conflict; docs/plan/16 §7.3 (co-founder register). Checked 2026-10-31.

The shape of it. A co-founder is a partner for ten years, chosen in a few months. The best ones come from people the founder has already worked with: a classmate on a hard project, a colleague from an internship, someone from a hackathon team or a club. Next best is someone met through working together on something small first. Matching platforms (YC co-founder matching, campus founder communities, Slack and Discord groups for builders) work when the founder treats the first conversation as the start of a trial, not a decision. The qualities that matter: complementary skills (someone who can build with someone who can sell), shared ambition and pace, honesty under stress, and a problem they both actually care about. Friendship helps and is not enough.

The first message. Peer to peer, not a pitch and not a job offer. One line on what you are building and why you, one line on why them specifically (what they built, wrote or said), and a twenty minute ask to compare notes on the problem. "I'm working on X and you spent two years inside the problem at Y; would you be up for twenty minutes to compare notes?" Under eighty words. Never "looking for a technical co-founder" as the subject line, never equity in the first message, never a long vision document attached.

The conversations. The first call is about the problem and about each other, not about terms. Over the next few weeks, work on something concrete together for two to four weeks: a prototype, customer interviews, a landing page and ten conversations. See how they handle disagreement, deadlines and bad news. Talk explicitly, early, about the things founders fight over: how much time each will commit and when they go full time, money and runway, roles and who decides what, equity, what happens if one wants out, where you will live and work, what success looks like. The YC co-founder questionnaire and the Founder Institute's list are good scripts. If a hard conversation is being avoided, that is the answer.

Equity and terms. Equal or near equal splits with four year vesting and a one year cliff are the norm for people starting together; a founder who has been working alone for a year with traction can justify more, and should say so plainly and early. Vesting protects everyone, including the person who stays. Put it in writing with a lawyer or a standard template before incorporating, and before any money comes in.

Who to talk to for advice. Founders who found their co-founder in the way the student is trying, founders who split with one (what they would have asked earlier), and anyone who has worked with the candidate. Ask the candidate for references too; it is normal.

What never happens: a co-founder chosen because they were available, equity promised in a first conversation, a "CEO" title handed out before anyone has done anything, a friendship assumed to survive without the hard conversations, or a search run only on a matching platform without doing something together first. Register: peer to peer, direct, candid, respectful of their time and their current commitments.
