# Management consulting recruiting

Sources: Management Consulted and the firms' own careers pages for timelines; docs/plan/15-outreach-playbook.md §3; packages/core/src/drafts/register.ts (questions by seniority). Checked 2026-10-31. Dates change every year and by office.

The timeline: undergraduate recruiting runs a year or more ahead and the deadlines are spread out by firm. For the junior summer internship, some firms close applications in the spring of sophomore year (McKinsey's business analyst intern around March, BCG's summer associate around June), others at the end of the summer before junior year (Bain's associate consultant intern in late August or early September), with interviews through the fall. Full-time applications for seniors close in July to September before senior year, and many full-time seats go to returning interns. [CHANGES YEARLY: check the firm's careers page or a deadline tracker for this year's date before stating one.] Sophomore, first-generation and diversity programs (names change by year) open in the fall and winter and often feed the junior summer class. Some firms recruit on campus at target schools with their own deadlines, so the school's career center calendar matters too. A student networking in the fall and winter of sophomore year is on time for the junior summer; one starting in October of junior year is late for the big three that cycle but fine for a smaller firm or for full-time.

The process: an online application with a resume and often a cover letter; an online assessment or game at some firms (McKinsey's Solve game, BCG's online case); first round interviews, usually two 30 to 45 minute case interviews with a fit section; then a final round of two or three more cases with partners and principals. A case interview is a business problem solved out loud with the interviewer, judged on structure, numbers, judgment and communication. The fit side matters as much: McKinsey calls its version the PEI (personal experience interview), three deep stories about leadership, drive and influence. Office choice happens at application: the student applies to one or two offices, so "which office and why" is a real question to prepare.

Who to talk to: consultants and business analysts one to three years in who share a school, club or hometown; associates and senior associates; an engagement manager or project leader for how staffing and case teams work. A partner only after an event, opening with what they said. Recruiters get a formal, logistics only note with one answerable question. Firms track who a candidate has spoken with and a consultant can flag an application, so the chats matter, but the referral is never asked for in message one.

What to ask: how they chose their office and what the first year is like there; how staffing works and how much choice they have; what case prep actually helped and what they would change; what separates the first-years who do well on a case team; how a student from a non-business major should prepare. Never ask a partner about their first year. Never ask about staffing from a banker or a group from a consultant.

Register: five sentences or fewer, a subject with school and their office or practice ("Cornell junior, your path to BCG Chicago"), "Hi" and first name for consultants and associates, full name, school and class year in the sign-off. One bump after five to seven business days, then stop. Name the office and the practice, not just the firm. The sequence is chat, thank-you within 24 hours, one or two light touches over two to eight weeks, then "I'm applying to the Chicago office this fall, would you be comfortable flagging my application when I submit?" Eight to twelve contacts per firm is normal, expecting around three replies in ten.

Case preparation: start eight to twelve weeks before interviews, learn the frameworks then abandon them, do thirty to fifty live practice cases with partners (classmates, a consulting club, alumni who offer), drill mental math, and prepare three fit stories that can be told in two minutes. Consultants will often offer a practice case on a call; take it up with a time.

Offers: consulting offers usually come with two to four weeks to decide and firms coordinate deadlines less than banks; ask for more time once, politely, with a date.
