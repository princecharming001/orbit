# Glossary

Sources: Wall Street Oasis and Mergers & Inquisitions glossaries; Y Combinator's SAFE primer; Carta's fundraising glossary; Management Consulted. Checked 2026-10-31. Plain words a sharp friend would use.

Recruiting. Superday: a bank's final round, several back to back interviews in one day, technical and behavioural. HireVue: a recorded video interview with timed answers, used as an early screen by banks and some companies. OA (online assessment): a timed test early in a tech, quant or consulting process; coding for engineers, probability and mental math for quant, a game or case for consulting. Summer analyst: a bank or asset manager's junior summer intern. Return offer: the full time offer given to a summer intern at the end of the summer. Off-cycle: an internship in the fall or spring rather than the summer, or recruiting outside the main calendar. Bulge bracket: the largest global banks (Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, Citi, Barclays, UBS, Deutsche Bank). Elite boutique: smaller advisory banks with large deals and no lending (Evercore, Lazard, Centerview, Moelis, PJT, Perella Weinberg, Qatalyst). Middle market: banks that advise on smaller deals (Houlihan Lokey, Jefferies, William Blair, Baird, Piper Sandler). Group: a bank's industry or product team (TMT, healthcare, FIG, M&A, leveraged finance); some banks place summer analysts by group. Coverage: the industry group that covers a set of companies. PEI: McKinsey's personal experience interview, the fit part of its interviews, three deep stories. Case interview: a business problem solved aloud with the interviewer, the core of consulting interviews. Fit interview: the behavioural half of an interview. MBB: McKinsey, Bain and BCG. Big Four: Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG. Target school: a school a firm recruits at on campus; non-target: one it does not. Networking call or coffee chat: a fifteen to twenty minute conversation about someone's path, the unit of recruiting networking. Informational interview: the formal name for the same thing. Referral: a current employee flagging a candidate to recruiting, which moves the application out of the pile. Req or requisition: a posting's internal ID. Exploding offer: an offer with a short deadline. Renege: accepting an offer and then backing out, which burns bridges. Target function: the kind of job the student is going for. Cycle: one recruiting season.

Investing jobs. Sourcing: finding companies to invest in. Diligence: checking a company before investing. Memo: the written case for an investment. Scout: a person, often a student or founder, who refers deals to a fund for a small cheque book or a bonus. Platform: a fund's non investing staff (community, talent, marketing). Analyst and associate: the junior investing roles; principal: senior, often leads deals but is not a partner; partner (GP, general partner): decides and sits on boards.

Fundraising. Pre-seed, seed, Series A: the first three rounds, from a few hundred thousand to tens of millions. SAFE: a simple agreement for future equity, money now for shares at the next priced round; post-money SAFE: the standard form, whose cap includes the SAFE money. Valuation cap: the maximum valuation at which a SAFE converts. Discount: a percentage off the next round's price for SAFE holders. Priced round: shares sold at an agreed valuation with a term sheet. Lead: the investor who sets terms and takes the largest share. Term sheet: the one page summary of the deal before legal documents. Pro rata: the right to invest in later rounds to keep your percentage. Dilution: the share of the company founders give up. Runway: months of cash left at the current burn. Burn: cash spent per month. Partner meeting: where a fund decides, usually weekly. Data room: a folder of documents investors review. Investor update: the monthly email to current and prospective investors. Angel: an individual who invests their own money. Accelerator: a program that invests a small amount in a cohort of companies (Y Combinator, Techstars). Carry: the share of profits a fund's partners earn. Cap table: who owns what. Vesting: shares earned over time, typically four years with a one year cliff.
