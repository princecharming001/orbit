# Investment banking recruiting

Sources: Wall Street Oasis and Mergers & Inquisitions recruiting timelines; docs/plan/15-outreach-playbook.md §3; packages/core/src/drafts/sector.ts (cycleTiming). Checked 2026-10-31. Exact dates change every year and by bank.

The timeline runs early, about 18 to 21 months before the internship, and it has kept moving earlier. Summer analyst recruiting for the junior summer happens during sophomore year: elite boutiques (Evercore, Moelis, PWP, Lazard, Centerview and the like) open applications in September to November of sophomore year, bulge brackets (Goldman, JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, Citi, Bank of America, Barclays, UBS) in December and January, with a trickle of middle market and regional banks into the spring. Applications are rolling, interviews start within weeks of a posting, and most seats are filled by late spring of sophomore year. [CHANGES YEARLY: search the web for a bank's current application open date before stating one; "Summer 2028 investment banking applications" finds the trackers.] So the networking window is the spring of freshman year through the fall of sophomore year, two to four months before each bank opens. A sophomore in October is on time but must move now; a junior writing about a summer internship is late: the right ask then is about off-cycle programs, smaller banks and boutiques that recruit closer to the summer, or the full-time path, and the question to a banker becomes "how you would approach recruiting from here if you were me", never "before recruiting starts".

Sophomore insight, "fast track" and diversity programs (names change: look them up) open in the late summer and fall of freshman and sophomore year, with deadlines in October, and often convert straight into junior summer interviews. Full-time recruiting outside of return offers is small; most full-time analysts come from the summer class, so the summer analyst seat is the goal. Off-cycle internships (fall or spring) exist at some banks and many boutiques and are a second door.

The process: an online application, often a HireVue (a recorded video interview with timed answers), first round calls with analysts and associates, then a superday: several back to back 30 minute interviews in one day with a mix of technical questions (accounting, valuation, the three statements, DCF, LBO basics) and behavioural ones ("walk me through your resume", "why banking", "why us", a deal you followed). Networking is what gets a resume pulled from the pile at most banks: referrals from analysts and associates in a group get looked at, and some groups effectively hire the people they have talked to.

Who to talk to: analysts and associates, alumni first, then people from your club, hometown or a shared employer. Managing directors and VPs are written to only with a hook. Recruiters and campus teams are for logistics questions only (deadlines, which group to apply to), in a formal note, one question. Banks hire by group at some firms (the student applies to a group, say Healthcare or TMT) and generalist at others; asking an analyst how their group works and how placement happens is a good question.

What to ask on a call: how they chose their group or their bank and what they wish they had known going in; what the first year looks like and how staffing works; what separates the summer analysts who do well; what to know before the superday. Never ask for a referral, an internship, "openings" or a resume review in a first message, and never whether the group "still has seats". A thank-you within 24 hours names one thing they said; analysts in a group compare notes, so no two get the same text.

Register: five sentences or fewer, a subject with school, class year and their group ("Michigan '28, quick question on Evercore healthcare"), "Hi" and first name for analysts and associates (they are two to four years older and read "Mr." as a culture misfit), "Mr. or Ms. Lastname" for VPs and above you have not met. Sign with full name, school and class year. Email from the .edu address Tuesday to Thursday, mid morning their time. Two bumps allowed a week apart, then move on. Volume is real in this sector: ten to twenty first messages a day in the networking window is normal, a few hundred people over a cycle from a target school and more from a non-target.

International students: most large US banks sponsor work visas for full-time analysts and take international students for summer roles on CPT; some boutiques and smaller firms do not. Sponsorship goes on the application when asked and never in a first networking message; ask about it on a call with an analyst in the same situation or with the recruiter late in the process.

Offers: an exploding offer is a real deadline, but most banks give a week or two more when asked promptly and politely, especially when another process is close to deciding. Ask the recruiter the same day by phone or email, say you are excited, and name the date you can decide by. Never accept and keep looking; a reneged offer burns the school and the student.
