# Finance beyond banking: private equity, equity research, sales and trading, asset management, corporate finance

Sources: Wall Street Oasis and Mergers & Inquisitions career guides for each path; packages/core/src/drafts/register.ts (private equity questions). Checked 2026-10-31. Dates change every year and by firm.

Private equity. Buyout and growth funds hire almost entirely from investment banking analysts after one to two years, through a headhunter driven process that now starts within months of an analyst's first day ("on cycle"), plus a smaller "off cycle" market later. A handful of large funds (Blackstone, KKR, Apollo, Carlyle, TPG, Bain Capital and a few others) run undergraduate analyst and summer analyst programs that recruit on the banking calendar, sophomore year, with applications in the late summer and fall. [CHANGES YEARLY: search the web for the fund's current program before stating when it opens.] For most students the honest answer is banking first, then PE. A student who wants PE asks a PE person how they made the move, what surprised them about the work, and how they would prepare for buy side recruiting as a student; someone senior is asked what they look for in the analysts they hire and what they would tell a student who wants to end up in private equity.

Equity research and sales and trading. Both sit inside banks and recruit on the bank's calendar, often through the same application portal as banking with a separate division choice, sometimes slightly later. Research wants writing, accounting and a view on a stock; a student should have one or two stock pitches ready. Sales and trading wants markets interest, quick numbers and a read on people; a student should follow markets daily and know why things moved this week. Both are smaller than banking and have shrunk over the years, so networking with people on the desk or in the coverage group matters more, and the question is about their desk or sector, never "your group" in the banking sense.

Asset management and hedge funds. Large asset managers (Fidelity, BlackRock, Vanguard, T. Rowe, Wellington, Capital Group, PIMCO) run summer analyst programs that post in the late summer and fall of the year before; hedge funds rarely take undergraduates except a few large multi-manager platforms with structured programs and the quant firms (see quant). A stock pitch or a view on a market is the currency in every conversation.

Corporate finance, FP&A, wealth management, commercial and corporate banking, insurance and fintech. Larger, later and more forgiving timelines: many post in the fall and spring of the year before and recruit on campus. These are good paths for students who are late for banking or who want finance with better hours, and the conversation should treat them as real choices, not fallbacks.

Who to talk to across these: analysts and associates one to three years in, alumni, people who made the move the student wants to make. Senior people only with a hook. Recruiters for logistics only.

Register: finance register. Five sentences or fewer, a subject with school, class year and their desk, sector or fund, "Hi" and first name for analysts and associates, "Mr. or Ms. Lastname" for senior people not yet met. Full name, school and class year in the sign-off. Up to two bumps a week apart in banking adjacent roles, one elsewhere. Email from the .edu address, Tuesday to Thursday mid morning.
