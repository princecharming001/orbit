# Networking etiquette

Sources: docs/plan/15-outreach-playbook.md (career center guides, practitioner forums, Dalton's "The 2-Hour Job Search"); Wall Street Oasis networking guides; Harvard OCS informational interview guide. Checked 2026-10-31.

The first message asks for insight, never for a job, a referral, a resume review or "any openings". It opens with the real connection (same school, a named referrer, an event they spoke at, a move they made) and carries one line that could only be true of this person. The ask is fifteen minutes, by phone or video, sometime in the next couple of weeks, phrased as a question with an easy out. Fifty to a hundred words. No resume attached to a first message; the one exception is a finance student with a relevant internship who mentions it in half a clause. Never "pick your brain", never "grab coffee sometime", never an hour.

Alumni reply at two to four times the rate of strangers, so alumni come first. Analysts, associates and engineers two to six years ahead reply far more than partners and VPs, and they are the ones who know what the process is like now. A senior person is only written to cold with a hook: they spoke on campus, they wrote something, someone sent you.

A coffee chat is a 15 to 20 minute call that the student runs. Open with a thirty second version of your story, then ask about their path and their team, what they look for, and what they wish they had known. Ask about their work, not about yours. End on time, offering to let them go, and close with "is there anyone else you'd suggest I talk to". A referral or an internship is never raised on the first call unless they bring it up. One chat per person before an interview invite; ask for a pointer to one more person instead of a second chat.

The thank-you goes out within 24 hours. It names when you spoke, one specific thing they said in their words, one thing you did or will do because of it, and asks whether it is alright to send a question later. Two people at the same firm never get the same text; they compare. Whoever introduced you is thanked the day it happens and told how the conversation went afterwards. That report back is the most skipped step and the one people remember.

Silence is normal. A quarter to a half of first messages get a reply, more from alumni, fewer from strangers. One bump after five to eight business days by email (ten to twelve on LinkedIn), two sentences, "in case it got buried", the ask in one clause, an out or a pointer to someone else. In finance a second bump a week later is fine; in consulting and tech one is enough. Never more than three unanswered messages to one person. After that let it rest and try someone else on the team.

An intro goes through a double opt-in. Ask the connector first with a two line forwardable blurb they can send as is: who you are, why this person, the specific ask, an out. The connector checks the other side is willing before connecting anyone. Never ask them to vouch for things they do not know.

Once a reply comes, answer the same day, within two business days at the outside. Propose two concrete windows with the time zone plus "or send me a time". Confirm the slot, the zone, the format and who calls whom, then send the invite. If they ask for something (a resume, which teams), answer that before anything else. Reschedule once, apologise once, offer two replacements.

Channels: email is better for alumni and anyone whose address you can find, LinkedIn for a first touch when you cannot and for people who are active there. Never the same message on both at once. A LinkedIn connection note is at most 300 characters (free accounts cut at 200) and must carry the ask itself; never a bare request and never "can I ask you something". When a LinkedIn reply comes, move to email to schedule. Orbit never sends a LinkedIn message itself; the student copies it over.

Send windows, finance and consulting: Tuesday to Thursday, mid morning in the recipient's time zone; avoid Monday 9am and Friday afternoon. Tech: within three days of a posting beats any window. Stay in touch every four to six weeks after a conversation with one real update tied to something they said and "no reply needed".

Words that mark a message as mail merge, never used: "I hope this finds you well", "reach out", "pick your brain", "leverage", "passionate about", "impressed by your background", "any advice you have", "touch base", "circle back".
