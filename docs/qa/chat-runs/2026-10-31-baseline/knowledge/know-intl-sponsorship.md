student: hey, im an international student on f-1 recruiting for banking in NYC. when i message bankers for the first time, should i mention that i'll need sponsorship or wait?
orbit:
I'd wait. A first message is for getting the chat, and leading with sponsorship can make it feel like you're asking for something before they know you. One thing first, though: check that the bank sponsors for IB analysts before you start reaching out, so you don't spend chats where it can't happen.

Once you're on a call and it's going well, it's fine to ask how the bank has handled it for other international analysts. Bankers who were on visas themselves are usually glad to talk about it, and alumni from outside the US are often the best people to ask. Never hide it on an application or from a recruiter, since that comes back to bite you.

student: ok that makes sense, thanks. how do i check which banks actually sponsor tho?
orbit:
Anytime. Start with each bank's careers page, since many say in their FAQ whether they sponsor for analyst roles. You can also ask the campus recruiting team at an info session or by email, where it's a normal question. Cornell's career office often keeps a list of employers that have hired international students.

To see who actually sponsors in practice, look at public H-1B data. The USCIS H-1B Employer Data Hub and sites built on the Labor Department's filings show which banks filed for analyst roles and how many. Talking to alumni who were on F-1 at a bank is the best check of all, since policies can change from year to year.
