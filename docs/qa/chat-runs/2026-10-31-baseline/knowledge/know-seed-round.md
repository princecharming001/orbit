Congrats on the term sheet, that's the hardest part.

The lead sets the terms: valuation or cap, round size, and the docs. They usually write the biggest check, do the real diligence, and often take a board seat in a priced round. student: ok makes sense. what's pro rata though? my lead mentioned wanting it and i dont really get what it means for me later
orbit:
Pro rata is the right to invest in your later rounds so they keep the same ownership. Say your lead ends up owning 15% after the seed. When you raise a Series A, they can buy enough of the new shares to stay at 15% instead of getting diluted.

It's standard, and a lead asking for it is a good sign because it means they expect to back you again. The catch comes later. Their pro rata takes up part of your Series A, and the new lead usually wants about 20% of the company, so if several seed investors hold pro rata, the round can get crowded. That's why many term sheets give it only to major investors above a set check size.

Two things to watch: "super pro rata," which lets them take more than their share, and a lead who passes on their pro rata later, since new investors notice that. Your lawyer can tell you whether yours is the plain kind.
